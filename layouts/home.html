You have probably done this

Most of a wardrobe is invisible from the front.

A wardrobe is a stack of fabric facing sideways. You reach past thirty things to the same five, and the rest may as well not be there. None of this is a fashion problem, and most people have never thought of it as solvable.

The thing behind the coat

You own something you like. It has been behind the winter coat since March, so it has not existed since March.

Two greys, one wrong

One of them works with the blue trousers. You will find out which after both are on.

Bought it twice

What it actually does

Your clothes, on your own body, before you get dressed.

Photograph each item once. It lives in a grid from then on, not on a rail.

Your body is a replica built from your own measurements, so a garment falls where it will actually fall - not where it falls on a model.

Weather and your calendar sit on the same screen, because both are settled before the wardrobe is opened.

The numbers

It is not just you.

About a fifth

The share of their wardrobe most people wear, most of the time. The rest is owned, paid for and unused.

Roughly a quarter

Of online clothing orders go back. Apparel returns run far higher than any other category.

Half to two thirds

Of those returns are fit and sizing. Not taste, not quality. The garment did not fit the body.

Most online shoppers

Now order more than one size intending to send the rest back. It was around four in ten in 2018.

Coresight and NRF on returns. McKinsey and Prime AI on fit as the cause. Repeated wardrobe studies on the fifth that gets worn.

Use cases

Ordinary days, and the ones with a trap in them.

Not styling. The practical parts: what is clean, what suits the weather, what the invitation actually meant, and what will not betray you under stage lights or in a photograph.
