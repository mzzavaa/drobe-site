## Right now: closed alpha

A small, invited group is using it. Closed on purpose - the point of an alpha is
to find the things that break on real wardrobes and real bodies, and that is
easier to do well with a few people you can actually talk to than with a few
thousand you cannot.

What alpha testers are doing: photographing their own clothes, building the
avatar from their own measurements, and telling us where the fit is wrong.

## Next: beta, and the waitlist is open

Beta opens the same build to a much wider group. If you want to be in it, join
the list. It is a list, not a queue with a number - we let people in as capacity
allows, and being early on it helps.

You are a good fit for the beta if any of these is true:

- You own more than you wear and you know it
- You struggle to tell whether two things go together
- You travel for work across climates, or pack for events with rules
- You buy secondhand and would buy more of it if judging fit were easier
- You want your body's actual measurements to be the thing that decides fit

You do not need to be interested in clothes. Most of the people this is built
for are not.

## What you are agreeing to

Plainly, because this involves photographs of your body:

- **The alpha stores everything on the device.** No account, no server, no upload.
- **Beta introduces accounts and cloud storage,** because a wardrobe that dies with a lost phone is not a product. What that means for your photographs will be written down before beta opens, not after.
- **You can leave and take nothing with you,** which is the correct default. Deleting the app deletes the data.

If that changes, it changes in a published document with a date on it, and beta
testers will be told rather than left to notice.

## What it will not be at beta

Beta is not the finished product. The [roadmap](/product/roadmap/) says what is
in each phase and roughly when. The short version: the basics work first, and
the more specialised the need, the later it arrives.

Being on the waitlist is not a commitment to anything. Neither is being in the
beta.
