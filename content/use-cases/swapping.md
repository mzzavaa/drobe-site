Nearly everyone has a bag by the door. It has been there a while. Inside are
things that are perfectly good and that somebody else would want.

It stays there because between "I should sell this" and actually doing it sits an
hour of photographing, measuring, describing and listing, per item. That hour is
why most of it eventually goes to landfill or, at best, gets dropped somewhere
without much thought.

## Where it stalls

{{< pair
  problem="**Listing takes longer than the item is worth.** Photograph it, find the light, measure it, write the description, pick the category. For a twelve-pound top."
  fix="It was photographed when it entered the wardrobe. The category and the measurements are already there. Listing becomes a decision, not a project." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**You do not know what is safe to let go of.** That jacket has not been worn in a year, but maybe next winter."
  fix="Wear counts are a fact rather than a feeling. Not worn in two winters is different from not worn since March, and you can see which it is." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**A clothes swap with friends, and everyone brings a bin bag** and nobody can see anything. It becomes rummaging, and most of it goes home again."
  fix="Everyone's items are already photographed. What is on offer can be seen before anyone carries anything anywhere." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Swapping only works if it fits**, and trying on at someone's kitchen table is exactly as awkward as it sounds."
  fix="Try it on your own avatar first. The swap becomes a decision made in advance rather than in a hallway." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Selling to a stranger means trusting the description.** Buyers get burned, so they discount everything, so sellers give up."
  fix="A listing that comes with real measurements and a try-on against the buyer's own body is a much stronger claim than four adjectives." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Handing something to a friend who would love it** requires remembering you own it and remembering they would."
  fix="A wardrobe you can see is a wardrobe you can offer from." >}}

## Trade, not only sale

Money is not always the point. A lot of what people want is a swap: something
they will wear for something they will not. That only works when both sides can
see what is on offer and judge whether it fits, which is the same problem this
whole product exists to solve.
