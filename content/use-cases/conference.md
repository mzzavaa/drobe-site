A conference is the hardest ordinary clothing problem most people face. It is
multi-day, you are photographed without warning, you are standing for most of it,
the room temperature is a lottery, and you have one bag.

{{< img src="photo-packing-on-bed.webp" caption="Four days planned as a set before anything goes in the bag. What is laid out is what survived the plan." >}}

## What makes it hard

You are not choosing one outfit. You are choosing a set that has to produce four
distinct-looking days from about six items, survive being sat in, walked in and
photographed, and fit in a cabin bag.

Doing that in your head, the night before, with the wardrobe half-invisible, is
why people over-pack and still feel underdressed on day three.

## The practical shape of a conference wardrobe

{{< steps >}}
1. **One colour base.** Trousers and shoes that work with every top you bring. This is what turns six items into four days.
2. **Layers, not alternatives.** A cardigan or jacket you can remove beats a second complete outfit for the same weight.
3. **One item that reads as deliberate.** Something with a bit of colour or structure for the day you present or the evening you are photographed.
4. **Nothing that needs ironing.** There is an iron in the room in theory. You will not use it.
5. **The shoes you have already walked nine hours in.** A conference is not where you find out about new shoes.
{{< /steps >}}
