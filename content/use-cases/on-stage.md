Dressing for a stage is not a style question. It is a set of technical
constraints, and almost nobody is told about them before their first talk. You
find out afterwards, from the video, or from the sound engineer's face.

{{< img src="concept-half-rendered-outfit.webp" caption="Half rendered, half wireframe. What a camera does to a garment is not what your mirror does." >}}

## The constraints, and why they exist

{{< pair
  problem="**Fine stripes or tight checks.** On camera they strobe and shimmer. It is called a moiré pattern and it is a property of the sensor, not of the shirt."
  fix="Note it on the item once. It stays noted. You never have to remember which shirt it was." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Pure white.** Blows out under stage lighting and drags the exposure down, so your face goes dark to compensate."
  fix="Off-white, light grey, or a soft colour. The item that is fine in a meeting room is not the item for a lit stage, and the two get told apart." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Nowhere to clip the lapel mic.** A crew-neck dress or a soft jumper gives the sound engineer nothing. This gets discovered five minutes before you go on."
  fix="A placket, a lapel, a collar or a firm neckline. It becomes part of what a stage outfit is, checked when the outfit is planned rather than in the wings." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Nowhere to put the battery pack.** A belt pack needs a waistband or a pocket. A dress with neither means it gets taped to you or hung somewhere awkward."
  fix="Planned as part of the outfit, not improvised. If the dress has no waistband the answer is a belt, and you brought one." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Necklaces, bangles, a lanyard.** Anything that swings will hit the lapel mic. It is inaudible to you and very audible in the recording."
  fix="The rule is attached to the accessory, not to your memory of the last time it happened." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Green, on a green screen.** Parts of you disappear. Same for blue on a blue screen."
  fix="Attached to the event: this venue keys on green, so nothing green." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Sweat.** Stage lights are hot and grey and light blue show everything."
  fix="Known per item. If it shows, it is not the stage shirt, and the app remembers that so you do not." >}}

## Why this belongs in a wardrobe app

Every one of these is a fact about a specific garment that you learn once and
then forget by the next time you speak. They are exactly the kind of thing that
should live with the item rather than in your head - the same way a care label
does.
