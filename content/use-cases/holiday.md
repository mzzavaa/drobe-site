Holiday packing is where people over-pack most and get caught out most, which
seems contradictory until you notice they are the same problem: you cannot see
what you are packing, so you pack quantity instead of a plan.

{{< img src="photo-bed-with-swatches.webp" caption="Half of what usually goes in the case never comes out of it." >}}

## The classics

{{< pair
  problem="Half the case never comes out. You wore the same three things and carried the rest around two countries."
  fix="Plan the days against what you will actually do. What goes in is what a day needs, not what might conceivably be needed." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="The hotel restaurant has a dress code. It is on the website, in a paragraph nobody read, and you are in shorts."
  fix="Dinners are events. An event has a dress code. It is decided before you pack, not at the door." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="A cathedral, a temple or a mosque on a hot day, in a vest and shorts. At the Grand Palace in Bangkok this happens to hundreds of visitors a day: turned away at the gate, then queuing in full sun to rent an ill-fitting cover-up. At the Vatican it happens after you have already waited hours."
  fix="One light long-sleeved layer and one scarf go in as a rule, not as an afterthought. They weigh almost nothing and they are the difference between going in and standing outside." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="Sunburn on day one in a strappy top, and every remaining top has straps."
  fix="Something with sleeves in the plan from the start. Day one sun changes what is wearable for the rest of the week." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="White linen, and then you sit on a painted bench, eat something with oil in it, or get caught in one shower."
  fix="You know which items are fragile because it is recorded on them, rather than rediscovered each holiday." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="Two weeks, no laundry plan, and everything after day eight is a compromise."
  fix="Fourteen days does not mean fourteen outfits. Plan the rotation and the wash, and the case halves." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="New sandals. Blisters by lunchtime on the first day, then trainers for a week in 30°C."
  fix="A holiday is not where you break in footwear. Obvious, and still the single most common trip-ruining packing mistake." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="The going-out outfit that only works with one specific bag and one specific pair of shoes, both of which stayed at home."
  fix="Seeing the full outfit assembled is what surfaces the missing piece - while you are still standing next to your wardrobe." >}}

## Places that will turn you away

Restaurants are the obvious one and the least common. These are the ones people
do not see coming.

{{< pair
  problem="**Monuments and heritage sites** with their own rules. Covered shoulders and knees at most religious sites, no bare feet, sometimes no hats indoors, sometimes shoes off entirely."
  fix="The rule belongs to the place, so it sits on the place. You see it while packing, not at the gate." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Clubs and bars with a door policy.** No shorts, no sportswear, no trainers, sometimes no visible logos. Berlin will refuse you for looking like a tourist and will not explain."
  fix="One outfit in the bag that clears a door policy. It is a small amount of space against not getting in." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Casinos, opera houses and some hotel bars** still hold a jacket rule after a certain hour, and will lend you a bad one."
  fix="Planned as an evening, in advance, rather than discovered at the entrance." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Rooftop bars and beach clubs** that read as casual and are not. Swimwear is fine at the pool and not at the table twenty metres away."
  fix="A cover-up that is an actual outfit rather than a towel. Decided while packing." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Boat trips, caves, volcanoes and anything with a safety briefing.** Closed shoes, long sleeves, nothing loose. Sandals will get you turned back at the jetty."
  fix="Activities are events too, with requirements that are practical rather than social." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Rented cover-ups.** Available at most sites, ill-fitting, warm, and photographed for the rest of your life."
  fix="One long-sleeved layer and one scarf weigh almost nothing and mean you never need one." >}}

## The evening problem

Most holiday packing is optimised for the day: beach, walking, sightseeing. The
evening is the afterthought, and it is the part with the rules - the dinner, the
bar with a door policy, the concert, the one night a photograph gets taken.

One considered evening outfit that clears a door beats four beach options you
will not miss.
