There is a specific kind of discomfort in walking into a room and knowing within
two seconds that you have got it wrong. Nothing is said. It does not need to be.

Dress codes are unwritten on purpose - they signal who already knows. That makes
them one of the few remaining ways to feel excluded at an event you were invited to.

{{< img src="concept-two-styles-blazer.webp" caption="The same event, two readings of what it asked for. Neither person was being careless." >}}

## The traps, and they are traps

{{< pair
  problem="**Smart casual.** Means a collar and no trainers to one host, and means jeans-with-a-nice-top to another. The invitation will not tell you which."
  fix="The dress code is stored on the event, and the outfit is planned against it days ahead - when there is still time to ask someone what they are wearing." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Wearing white to a wedding.** Everyone knows this one. Fewer people know that cream, ivory, champagne and a heavily white-patterned dress all count."
  fix="Plan the outfit against the event rather than the morning. You look at it days early, which is when someone can tell you." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Black at a christening or a naming ceremony.** Correct in some cultures, quietly wrong in several others."
  fix="An event carries its own note. The reason a thing is a bad idea is attached to the event, not left in your memory." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**A church, a temple or a mosque on a hot day.** Shoulders and knees covered. You find out at the door, in front of the queue."
  fix="Planning the outfit against a place, not just a temperature, means a scarf or a layer is part of the plan rather than a purchase at the entrance." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Sitting down.** The skirt that is fine standing up and not fine on a low sofa. The shirt that gapes only when you reach."
  fix="You saw the whole outfit on a body before you left, from the front, at full length." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Sheer under bright light.** The trousers that are opaque in your bedroom and not opaque in a bright venue, or in a photograph with a flash."
  fix="Items get noted once and stay noted. You do not have to remember which ones betray you." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**A colour that changes under the venue's lighting.** One guest was asked to leave a wedding over a dress she describes as bumblebee yellow. Under the venue's black lights it read as white."
  fix="Lighting is a property of the venue, and it belongs on the event alongside the dress code - the same way a stage's green screen does." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Overdressing, which is also a failure.** A first day in business casual with a tie, and the manager asking you not to dress like that again because it made everyone else look bad."
  fix="The dress code is recorded from what people actually wear, not from what the invitation implies. Both directions are wrong, and only one of them gets talked about." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Finding out at the door.** One guest realised the error on arrival and spent the reception in a back staircase rather than walk into the room."
  fix="The whole point of planning against the event days ahead is that this is discovered while it is still fixable." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="**Something visible in every photograph and nobody said anything.** A bride found her bra plainly visible in almost all of her wedding photos, seen only afterwards."
  fix="Seeing the full outfit on a body, front on, at full length, is when that shows up - not in a bathroom mirror at chest height." >}}

## The point

Not to make you fashionable. To stop the specific, avoidable moment where you
are the only person in the room who did not know.

{{< note >}}
**Real in the demo.** Events with dress-code labels, weather, and a plan-outfit
slot per event. **Planned:** the outfit actually attached to the event, and the
stylist that reads the dress code and suggests from what you own.
{{< /note >}}
