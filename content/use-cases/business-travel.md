Leaving 6°C and rain, landing in 29°C and humidity, with a client meeting three
hours after you get off the plane. Then doing it again in the other direction on
Thursday.

The wardrobe problem here is not variety. It is that two incompatible sets of
requirements have to fit in one bag, and the requirements are not only about
temperature.

{{< img src="concept-weather-split.webp" caption="The same trip, both ends. Dressing for one of them is how people end up carrying a coat for four days." >}}

## Climate

{{< pair
  problem="You dress for the destination and freeze in the departure airport, or dress for home and land in a coat you now carry for four days."
  fix="Both ends are on the calendar with their own weather. The layer you remove on the plane is a planned item, not a mistake." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="29°C and humid, and the linen shirt that works in dry heat is transparent when damp."
  fix="Humidity is a different problem from temperature, and the item that solves one does not solve the other. Noted per item, per destination." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="Air conditioning indoors is 18°C while it is 34°C outside. You are dressed for one of them all day."
  fix="Planned as indoor and outdoor rather than as a single temperature. The cardigan is on the list because the meeting room is cold." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="Rain that arrives at four every afternoon in the tropics, which is not the same as rain in Manchester."
  fix="Shoes that dry, not shoes that are waterproof, is a different decision - and one made at packing time rather than in a doorway." >}}

## Local norms

This is the part that has nothing to do with weather and catches people hardest,
because getting it wrong reads as disrespect rather than as a wardrobe error.

{{< pair
  problem="Shoulders and knees covered for a site visit, a temple, a mosque, or a government building - and you brought a sleeveless dress because it was 34°C."
  fix="The constraint sits on the destination, alongside the weather, so it is visible when you pack rather than at the door." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="Business formal in one office and pointedly informal in another. Turning up in a suit can be as wrong as turning up without one."
  fix="Each meeting is its own event with its own dress code, so one trip can hold two different answers." >}}

{{< pair
  problem="Shoes that come off at the door, worn with the socks you have had on since a 6am flight."
  fix="A small thing, entirely avoidable, and only avoidable if you thought about it before you packed." >}}

## The bag

{{< steps >}}
1. **One shoe colour.** It is the heaviest thing you pack and the hardest to double up.
2. **A base that works in both climates.** Usually trousers in a mid-weight fabric that is neither hot nor thin.
3. **Layers for the difference,** not two separate wardrobes.
4. **The modest option, always.** One long-sleeved layer that covers shoulders, regardless of forecast. It weighs nothing and it is the one you will need.
5. **Meeting-ready on arrival.** Whatever you wear on the plane is the outfit for the first meeting, because your bag might not arrive.
{{< /steps >}}

{{< note >}}
**Real in the demo.** Weather by city on the events screen, with the location
editable. **Planned:** multi-day trips, per-destination constraints, and the
packing list that falls out of a planned set of days.
{{< /note >}}
