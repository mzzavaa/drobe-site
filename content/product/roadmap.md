## Why this order

**The simple thing has to be right before the hard thing is worth attempting.**
If a garment does not render honestly on your body, nothing built on top of it
is worth having. So the first real milestone is not a feature, it is a garment
looking the way it will actually look.

**Menswear first is about variables, not about audience.** Where convention
allows a narrower set of shapes, there are fewer ways for a render to be subtly
wrong, and it is easier to tell whether it is working. Draped garments, hems
that fall differently on different bodies, and layered outfits are harder
problems, and shipping them badly is worse than shipping them later. The work
carries straight over; it is a sequencing decision.

**A body that changes is not an edge case.** It applies across a cycle, through
pregnancy and after it, through weight change, illness, injury, and simply
time. Almost nobody has one fixed body, so a product that assumes one is wrong
about almost everyone. It arrives once the wardrobe side is solid, because it
needs the fit underneath it to already be dependable.

**Layering is last because it is hardest.** A layered outfit is not several
garments displayed together, it is garments sitting over and under each other
correctly. It is also what makes packing and travel genuinely work, so it is
worth waiting for rather than faking.

## What this page does not say

The specifics of how the avatar is built, how try-on is rendered, and what makes
it hold up better than the alternatives are deliberately not here. The order and
the timing are useful to you. The method is not, and it is the part worth
keeping.
